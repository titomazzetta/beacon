# Radiation, Chemical and Pandemic Card

## Nuclear blast or radiation release
**Get inside. Stay inside. Stay tuned.**
- Go to a basement, or the middle of a sturdy brick or concrete building, away from the outside walls and the roof. A car is not a shelter.
- **Stay inside at least 24 hours** unless officials say otherwise. Fallout gets much weaker over the first day.
- If you were outside: take off your outer layer of clothing as soon as you're in (that alone removes up to **90%** of the radioactive material), bag it, and put the bag away from people. Then wash: shower if you can (shampoo, no conditioner), or wipe skin and hair with a wet cloth. Gently blow your nose and wipe your eyelids and ears.
- Brush or wash pets that were outside.
- Food and water in sealed containers inside the building are safe.
- **Potassium iodide (KI)** only protects the thyroid from radioactive iodine. Take it only when public health officials tell you to.
- Listen to a battery or crank radio for instructions (see the Radio card). *Nuclear War Survival Skills* in the library covers expedient shelters in depth.

## Chemical release
- If told to shelter in place: get inside now. Close and lock windows and doors, close fireplace dampers, and turn off fans, heating and air conditioning.
- Go to an interior room **above the ground floor** with the fewest windows and vents. If officials advise it, seal gaps around the door, windows and vents with plastic sheeting and duct tape.
- If you're outside: get away from the source, upwind and uphill. Cover your nose and mouth.
- If exposed: take off contaminated clothing (cut it off rather than pull it over your head), wash skin with plenty of soap and water, and rinse eyes with plain water for 10 to 15 minutes. Call 911, or Poison Control in the US at **1-800-222-1222**.

## Pandemic or serious outbreak
- Stay home when sick. One person cares for the sick person, in a separate room (and bathroom) if possible.
- Wear a well-fitting mask (N95 or similar) around sick people and in crowded indoor spaces. Open windows for fresh air. Wash hands often.
- Keep two weeks of food, water and medicines; refill prescriptions early.
- Get urgent care for trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, or bluish lips or face.

Sources: CDC radiation emergencies ("Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned"); Ready.gov; OSHA shelter-in-place guidance; CDC chemical emergencies; Nuclear War Survival Skills (Kearny, Oak Ridge National Laboratory).
