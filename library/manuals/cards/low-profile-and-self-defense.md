# Low Profile & Personal Safety Card

## Awareness is the best defense
Use the "color code" for your state of mind:
- **White**: oblivious (phone, headphones). Avoid in public during a crisis.
- **Yellow**: relaxed but aware. You know where the exits are and who's around. **Live here.**
- **Orange**: something's off. Pick a plan: leave, avoid, prepare.
- **Red**: act on the plan.

Trust the feeling that something is wrong. Leaving early costs nothing.

## Blend in (the "gray man")
- Dress like the locals and the moment: plain, neutral colors. **No military-style or "tactical" gear, no slogans or political symbols.**
- Don't show cash, jewelry, expensive gear, or your supplies.
- **Don't talk or post about your preparations.** In a long emergency, the house with lights, noise and food smells draws attention. Keep generator use, lights and cooking discreet.
- Move with the normal flow of people and traffic, in daylight, without hurrying. Have a simple, true reason for where you're going.
- Carry bags that look ordinary: a backpack, not a military pack.

## Avoid, then escape, then defend
1. **Avoid**: stay away from crowds, protests, checkpoints, dark shortcuts.
2. **De-escalate**: calm voice, open hands, give space, don't argue, offer a way out.
3. **Comply with robbery**: wallets, phones and cars can be replaced. You can't.
4. **Escape**: if you can run, run. Make noise. Go toward people and light.
5. **Defend** only when you can't get away. Commit fully, aim for the eyes, nose, throat and groin, then escape as soon as you can. The goal is getting away, not winning.

## Know the law where you are
Self-defense law differs a lot between states and countries: duty to retreat, "stand your ground," what counts as reasonable force, and weapon carry rules. Laws still apply in an emergency. Learn yours ahead of time.

## At home
- Light the outside, lock doors and windows, trim bushes near entrances. A dog is an excellent alarm.
- Pick a **safe room** with a solid door, a phone and a light. Agree with your family on what everyone does if someone breaks in: go to the safe room and call 911.
- Know your neighbors. Groups that look out for each other are far safer than any one household.

## Traveling through unsafe areas
- Plan the route; know where the safe places are (hospitals, police, embassies, busy hotels).
- Keep a small "giveaway" wallet with some cash; hide the rest in two or three places.
- Share your route and check-in times with someone. Agree on a code word that means "I'm in trouble."

Sources: Jeff Cooper's color code of awareness; US Army FM 3-50.3 Survival, Evasion and Recovery; FBI / DHS personal safety guidance; Ready.gov.
