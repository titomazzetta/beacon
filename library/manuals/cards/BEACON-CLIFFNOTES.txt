1. FIRST: STOP
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  STOP - THINK - OBSERVE - PLAN.  Panic kills more people than most
  hazards. Take three slow breaths: two quick sniffs in through the
  nose, one long breath out. Then decide.

  RULE OF THREES (rough limits for a person):
    3 minutes without air (or with severe bleeding)
    3 hours without shelter in harsh cold, heat or wet
    3 days without water
    3 weeks without food
  So the order is usually: SAFETY > FIRST AID > SHELTER/WARMTH >
  WATER > SIGNAL FOR HELP > FOOD.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. LIFE-SAVING FIRST AID
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  NOT BREATHING NORMALLY: call 911, then CPR. Push hard and fast in
    the center of the chest, at least 2 inches deep, 100-120 pushes a
    minute. Hands-only is fine. Use an AED as soon as one arrives.
  SEVERE BLEEDING: press hard on it and don't let go. Pack deep wounds
    with cloth. Arm or leg bleeding you can't stop: tourniquet 2-3
    inches above the wound (not on a joint), tighten until it stops,
    write the time, leave it on.
  CHOKING: 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts, repeat. Babies: back
    blows + chest pushes, never abdominal thrusts.
  BURNS: cool running water 10-20 minutes. No ice or butter.
  STROKE (FAST): Face droop, Arm weak, Speech slurred -> Time to call.
  ALLERGIC REACTION: epinephrine auto-injector in the outer thigh, call.
  HEAT STROKE (hot, confused): cool them NOW with water, ice, shade.
  HYPOTHERMIA (shivering, slurring, clumsy): get dry, insulate, warm
    the core, sweet warm drinks only if fully awake.
  SNAKEBITE: keep still and calm, limb at heart level, get to a
    hospital. No tourniquet, cutting, sucking or ice.
  BROKEN BONE: splint it as found; check fingers/toes stay warm/pink.
  WOUNDS: rinse with lots of clean water, cover, change daily. Spreading
    redness, red streaks or fever = infection, get antibiotics/help.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WATER  (1 gallon / 4 liters per person per day)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  BOIL: rolling boil 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft / 2,000 m).
  BLEACH (plain, unscented): 6% bleach = 8 drops per gallon;
    8.25% bleach = 6 drops per gallon. Double if cloudy (filter first
    if you can). Wait 30 minutes; it should smell slightly of chlorine.
  HIDDEN WATER AT HOME: water heater tank, ice, toilet TANK (not bowl,
    no cleaner tablets), canned food liquid, pipes (open top faucet,
    drain from the lowest).
  Boiling and bleach kill germs but do NOT remove fuel or chemicals.
  DEHYDRATION DRINK: 1 liter clean water + 6 level tsp sugar +
    1/2 level tsp salt.
  NEVER drink seawater or urine.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SHELTER & WARMTH
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Get out of WIND and WET first. Insulate from the ground: lying on
  cold ground steals heat faster than air does. Use leaves, pine
  boughs, cardboard, a pad.
  Dress in layers: wicking base, warm middle, wind/rain shell. Cotton
  kills when wet. Hat, gloves, dry socks.
  At home in a winter outage: pick one interior room, close it off,
  hang blankets over windows and doors, everyone sleeps there.
  CARBON MONOXIDE: never run a generator, grill, camp stove, or car
  indoors or in a garage. Generators at least 20 feet from the house.
  Headache, dizziness, nausea in several people = get outside now.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. FIRE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Needs HEAT + FUEL + AIR. Gather everything before you strike:
    TINDER (dry grass, birch bark, cotton balls with petroleum jelly,
    dryer lint) -> KINDLING (pencil-thin dry twigs) -> FUEL (wrist-thick
    and up). Dead branches still on trees are drier than ground wood.
  Build small, feed it gradually, protect it from wind. Clear the area
  to bare soil. Never leave a fire alone; drown it and stir it cold.
  Carry two ways to start fire: lighter + ferro rod, in a dry bag.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SIGNALING FOR RESCUE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  THREE of anything = distress: 3 whistle blasts, 3 fires, 3 flashes.
  SOS in Morse: ... --- ...  (light, horn, whistle, tapping).
  Ground signals (big, 10 ft / 3 m): V = need help, X = need medical
  help, N = no, Y = yes, arrow = going this way.
  Mirror or phone screen: aim the flash through a V made by your other
  hand. Bright cloth, smoke by day, fire by night.
  If people know where you are and when you're due, STAY PUT.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
7. NAVIGATION WITHOUT GPS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Sun rises roughly east, sets roughly west; at midday it's due south
  in the northern hemisphere.
  SHADOW STICK: mark the shadow tip, wait 15-30 min, mark again.
  First mark = west, second = east.
  NIGHT: Big Dipper's end-of-cup stars point to the North Star
  (about 5x the gap). North Star = north.
  WATCH (N. hemisphere): hour hand at the sun; south is halfway to 12
  (to 1 during daylight saving time).
  Moss is NOT a reliable compass.
  Walking: ~5 km (3 mi) per hour on flat ground, +1 hour per 600 m
  (2,000 ft) of climb. Count paces: ~60-70 double steps per 100 m.
  Following water downhill often leads to people (watch for cliffs).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
8. FOOD
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  You can go weeks without food; don't take risks to get it.
  NEVER eat wild mushrooms unless an expert identified them in person.
  NEVER eat: white umbrella-shaped flower clusters (hemlock), milky
  sap, bitter/soapy taste, almond smell, yew, "onions" that don't
  smell like onion.
  Safe-ish & common (confirm with a regional guide): dandelion,
  cattail, blackberries/raspberries, clover, plantain, acorns (leached).
  GAME: don't eat sick-acting animals. Bear and wild pig: cook to
  160 F / 71 C. Fish: 145 F / 63 C. Gloves for rabbits.
  Store food cool, dry, dark; rotate it. 2,000-2,500 calories per adult
  per day.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
9. RADIO & COMMUNICATIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  NOAA Weather Radio: 162.400-162.550 MHz (7 channels).
  CB Channel 9 (27.065 MHz) = emergency. CB 19 = highway.
  GMRS Channel 20 (462.675 MHz) = common emergency channel (license).
  Ham 2-meter calling: 146.520 MHz. Marine VHF Ch 16: 156.800 MHz.
  In a life-threatening emergency, US law lets anyone use any radio.
  DISTRESS CALL: "MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY" - who - WHERE - what's wrong -
  how many - what you need. Repeat every few minutes.
  Texts often get through when calls don't.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
10. SEVERE WEATHER
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  WATCH = get ready.  WARNING = act now.
  TORNADO: lowest floor, interior room, no windows, cover your head.
    Mobile homes are not safe. Not under overpasses.
  LIGHTNING: when thunder roars, go indoors; wait 30 min after the last
    thunder. Count flash-to-bang seconds / 5 = miles.
  FLOOD: Turn Around, Don't Drown. 12 inches of moving water floats a
    car; 6 inches can knock you down.
  HURRICANE: evacuate when told; storm surge kills the most people.
  HEAT + HUMIDITY: drink before thirsty, work early or late, never
    leave kids or pets in cars. Fans alone don't work above ~95 F.
  EARTHQUAKE: Drop, Cover, Hold On. Not in a doorway.
  WILDFIRE: leave early. Smoke: stay in, N95 masks outside.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
11. POWER OUTAGE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Fridge stays cold ~4 hours closed; full freezer ~48 hours.
  Throw out perishables above 40 F / 4 C for 2+ hours.
  NEVER plug a generator into a wall outlet (it can kill line workers).
  Use extension cords to appliances, or an electrician-installed
  transfer switch.
  Drip faucets to prevent frozen pipes. Unplug electronics for surges.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
12. CAR
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Stranded in snow: stay with the car, CLEAR THE TAILPIPE, run engine
  10 min/hour with a window cracked, bright cloth on the antenna.
  Car in water: Seatbelts off, Window open/break (side window),
  Out, Children first.  Keep a window-breaker tool in reach.
  Jump start: red to dead +, red to good +, black to good -, black to
  bare metal on the dead car.
  Overheating: heater on full, pull over, never open a hot radiator.
  Keep the tank half full in storm season or unrest.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
13. EVACUATION & LEAVING THE COUNTRY
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Decide your triggers in advance. LEAVE EARLY.
  Go-bag per person: water, 3 days food, meds, light, radio, phone +
  battery, cash in small bills, documents, warm layer, paper map.
  Documents: passports, IDs, birth/marriage certificates, deeds,
  insurance, medical records, prescriptions - originals in a
  waterproof bag, copies on paper and an encrypted USB.
  Three routes out; avoid bridges, tunnels, interstates. Three rally
  points (home, neighborhood, out of town). One out-of-area contact.
  Leaving the country: passport valid 6+ months; know your
  citizenships and residency rights; official crossings only; US
  citizens leave/enter the US on a US passport; enroll in STEP abroad.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
14. STAYING SAFE AROUND PEOPLE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Stay aware ("yellow"): know exits, watch hands, trust bad feelings.
  Blend in: plain clothes, no tactical gear, no slogans, don't show
  supplies or cash, don't talk about your preps.
  Avoid -> de-escalate -> comply with robbery -> escape -> defend only
  if you can't get away. Know your local self-defense laws.
  Firearms: treat every gun as loaded; never point at what you won't
  destroy; finger off trigger until ready; know what's beyond the
  target. Lock guns away from children.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
15. MIND & BODY
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Routine beats panic: same wake time, water, movement, meals, chores,
  news at set times only, connection, sleep.
  Box breathing: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4.
  Sleep 7-9 hours if you possibly can; judgment fails first.
  Daily: 20 minutes of squats, push-ups, lunges, planks, walking.
  Control what you can; let go of what you can't.
  Watch each other for hopelessness, not eating or sleeping, or talk
  of not wanting to live - stay with them and get help.
