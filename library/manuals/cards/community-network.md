# Community Network and Mutual Aid Card

For your street, building or camp: how people look after each other when outside help is slow to come.

## Start before you need it
- Meet your neighbors now. Write down who lives where, who has medical needs, kids, elders or pets, and who has useful skills: nurse, electrician, mechanic, ham operator, hunter, gardener. Keep the list private and printed.
- Pick a meeting place (a driveway, a church lot, a school) and a backup.
- Agree on a signal that a household needs help: a white or orange cloth on the door, three whistle blasts, or HELP on the Beacon Board or the mesh.

## Roles (one person can hold several)
- **Lead**: runs a short daily meeting and writes decisions down.
- **Medical**: the first aid kit, and who needs medicines or powered medical devices.
- **Comms**: radios, mesh radios, the check-in schedule, the Beacon Board.
- **Supplies**: water, food, fuel, power stations; who has what to share.
- **Watch**: the security rotation (see the Perimeter card).
- **Kids and elders**: a calm space, routines, daily checks on people living alone.

## Check-ins
- At set times every day (for example 8 a.m. and 8 p.m.), every household reports **Safe** or **Needs help**.
- On Beacon's Board, tap "We're safe" or "We need help". From a Meshtastic radio, send **SAFE** and your name, or **HELP your name: what you need**.
- If a household misses two check-ins, two people go and knock.

## Communications plan (PACE)
Write down four ways to reach each other, in order:
1. **Primary**: phones and texts, while they work.
2. **Alternate**: Beacon over the Wi-Fi or a phone hotspot, and its Board.
3. **Contingency**: walkie-talkies on one agreed channel and one backup channel (FRS needs no license; GMRS needs a license but no exam), and Meshtastic text radios.
4. **Emergency**: runners, whistles, signs on doors, and the meeting place at set times.
- Keep radio talk short. Anyone can listen: never broadcast addresses, supplies, or when a house will be empty.

## Sharing and trade
- Pool what helps everyone: water filters, power stations for phones and medical devices, generators, tools, chainsaws.
- Keep small bills and coins. Things people trade for: batteries, fuel, water treatment, over-the-counter medicine, coffee, hygiene supplies.
- Write down what was lent to whom.

## Keep it safe and fair
- Decide rules together: who gets help first (medical needs, kids, elders), and how disagreements get settled.
- Stay within the law: don't block public roads or take anyone's property. Hand over to fire, medical and police crews when they arrive, and tell them who needs help.
- Watch each other for exhaustion, heat or cold stress, and despair. Rotate hard jobs.

Sources: FEMA Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) program; Ready.gov; American Red Cross; Meshtastic documentation; FCC rules for FRS and GMRS (47 CFR Part 95). This card is a starting point; take CERT training if it's offered near you.
