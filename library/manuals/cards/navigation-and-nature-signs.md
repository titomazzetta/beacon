# Navigation Without GPS Card

## Lost? STOP first
**S**top · **T**hink · **O**bserve · **P**lan. Most people get "more lost" by moving fast in panic. If someone knows where you are and when to expect you, **staying put** usually gets you found faster.

## Find north by day
- **Sun**: rises roughly east, sets roughly west. At midday (about 1 pm during daylight saving time) it's due **south** if you're north of the tropics, due **north** in the southern hemisphere.
- **Shadow stick** (about 15–30 minutes):
  1. Push a stick upright into flat ground. Mark the tip of its shadow with a stone. **This first mark is west.**
  2. Wait 15–30 minutes. Mark the new shadow tip. **This second mark is east.**
  3. A line from the first to the second mark runs west → east. Stand with the first mark on your left: you're facing **north**.
- **Analog watch** (northern hemisphere): point the hour hand at the sun. **South** is halfway between the hour hand and 12 (use 1 instead of 12 during daylight saving time). Southern hemisphere: point 12 at the sun; halfway between 12 and the hour hand is **north**.

## Find north by night
- **North Star (Polaris)**: find the Big Dipper. The two stars on the end of the "cup" point to Polaris, about 5 times the distance between them. Polaris sits almost exactly over true north, and its height above the horizon roughly equals your latitude.
- **Southern hemisphere**: extend the long axis of the Southern Cross about 4½ times its length; straight below that point is south.
- **Moon**: if it rises before the sun has set, its lit side faces west. If it rises after midnight, its lit side faces east.

## Don't trust
- **Moss on the north side of trees.** Moss grows wherever it's shady and damp. It's not reliable.

## Using a compass properly
- A compass points to **magnetic** north, not true north. The difference (**declination**) depends on where you are and is printed on most paper maps.
- **True bearing = magnetic bearing + east declination** (subtract if the declination is west).
- Hold the compass flat and away from metal, phones, cars and power lines.

## Distance and time
- **Pace count**: count every time your left foot hits the ground. Most adults take about 60–70 of these double paces per 100 m on flat ground. Measure yours before you need it.
- **Walking time (Naismith's rule)**: 1 hour per 5 km (3 miles), plus 1 hour for every 600 m (2,000 ft) of climb. Add more for rough ground, heavy packs, kids or darkness.
- **Hand at arm's length**: each finger width between the sun and the horizon is roughly 15 minutes of daylight left.

## Follow the land
- Water flows downhill toward bigger water, and people often live near rivers and coasts. Following a stream downhill often leads to roads or settlements, but avoid cliffs and waterfalls.
- **Handrails**: follow long features like ridges, power lines, fences, rivers or roads. Aim to one side of a target ("aim off") so you know which way to turn when you hit the handrail.

## Finding water
- Low ground, valleys and the outside bends of dry streambeds (dig there). Bright green plants, cattails, willows and cottonwoods. Animal trails heading downhill, especially at dawn and dusk. Birds circling low; insects swarming.
- **Never drink** seawater or urine. Treat all wild water before drinking (see the Water card).
