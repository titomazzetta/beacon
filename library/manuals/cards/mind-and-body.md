# Mind & Body Card

Stress is normal in a crisis. A steady mind makes better decisions, and it's trainable.

## Calm down in under a minute
- **Physiological sigh**: two quick inhales through the nose (the second tops up your lungs), then one long, slow exhale through the mouth. Repeat 2–3 times.
- **Box breathing**: in for 4 counts, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Do 4 rounds.
- **5-4-3-2-1 grounding**: name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.

## Anchor the day (routine fights panic)
Wake at the same time → water → move your body → eat → check news or radio at set times only → chores and work on the plan → connect with people → wind down → sleep. Write tomorrow's top 3 tasks before bed.

## Sleep
Same schedule every day. Dark, cool, quiet. If you can't get 7–9 hours, a 20-minute nap helps. Lack of sleep wrecks judgment faster than hunger.

## 20-minute no-equipment workout (3–4 rounds, rest 1 minute between)
- 15 squats · 10 push-ups (knees or against a wall is fine) · 10 lunges each leg
- 30-second plank · 15 glute bridges · 10 burpees or 30 seconds of jumping jacks
Warm up 3 minutes first. Walk daily. Carry heavy things (water jugs) for strength.

## Food and water
Adults usually need roughly 2,000–2,500 calories a day, more when working hard or cold. Drink regularly; pale-yellow urine means you're hydrated. Protein and fat keep you full longer.

## Discipline in hard times
- Do one small hard thing first every morning. Small wins build momentum.
- Separate what you control from what you don't. (This is the core of Stoic philosophy: the Meditations and Enchiridion are on the Reading Shelf.)
- Plan, then act. Revisit the plan at a set time each day, not every five minutes.

## Taking care of each other
- **Kids**: keep routines, tell the truth simply, let them help with real tasks, make time for play. Watch for regression (bedwetting, clinginess); it's common and usually passes with comfort.
- Check in with each other daily: "How are you really doing?"
- Grief and fear come in waves. Talking, writing, praying, or meditating all help.

## Warning signs: get help
Not sleeping for days, not eating, hopelessness, heavy drinking or drug use, talk of not wanting to live, or not being able to function. Stay with the person. When phones work: in the US, call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or 911 for an emergency.

## Meaning and faith
People who survive long ordeals often credit purpose: someone to get home to, faith, a duty. Prayer, meditation, gratitude, and helping others all strengthen resilience. The Reading Shelf includes the Bible, Bhagavad Gita, Tao Te Ching, Dhammapada and Qur'an.

Sources: US Army FM 7-22 Holistic Health and Fitness; National Center for PTSD Psychological First Aid; Stanford research on cyclic sighing (Balban et al., 2023); 988 Lifeline.
