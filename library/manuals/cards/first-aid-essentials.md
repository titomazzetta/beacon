# Emergency First Aid Card

**Call 911 (US) or 112 (Europe and many mobile networks) first if you can.** Send someone else to call while you help. If you're alone with an adult who collapsed, call first, then start CPR.

## 1. Check: Is it safe? Are they breathing?
- Make sure you won't get hurt too (traffic, fire, electricity, gas, water).
- Tap and shout. Look for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is NOT normal breathing.

## 2. Not breathing normally → CPR
1. Lay them on their back on a firm surface.
2. Heel of one hand on the center of the chest, other hand on top.
3. Push **hard and fast**: at least **2 inches (5 cm)** deep, **100–120 pushes a minute** (the beat of "Stayin' Alive"). Let the chest come all the way back up.
4. Untrained or unwilling to give breaths? **Hands-only CPR** is fine for adults. Trained: 30 pushes, 2 breaths.
5. Use an **AED** as soon as one arrives: turn it on and follow its voice.
6. Don't stop until help takes over, they start breathing, or you physically can't go on.
- Children: same idea, about 2 inches deep. Infants: two fingers, about 1.5 inches deep.

## 3. Severe bleeding
1. **Press hard** directly on the wound with cloth, gauze or your hand. Don't let up to look.
2. Deep wound in a leg, arm, armpit or groin? **Pack it**: stuff gauze or clean cloth into the wound, then press hard.
3. **Tourniquet** for life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg: **2–3 inches above the wound**, not on a joint. Tighten until the bleeding stops. Write down the time. Don't loosen or remove it; that's for the hospital.

## 4. Choking (can't talk, cough or breathe)
- Adult or child: **5 back blows** between the shoulder blades, then **5 abdominal thrusts** (fist just above the belly button, pull in and up). Repeat until it comes out.
- Infant: 5 back blows face-down on your forearm, then 5 chest pushes with two fingers. **No abdominal thrusts on babies.**
- If they go unconscious: lower them down, call 911, start CPR. Look in the mouth before breaths and remove anything you can see.

## 5. Burns
- Cool with **cool running water for 10–20 minutes**. No ice, butter or ointments.
- Remove rings and tight clothing before swelling starts. Don't pull off stuck clothing.
- Cover loosely with clean plastic wrap or a non-stick dressing.
- Get medical help for: burns bigger than their palm, on the face, hands, feet, groin or joints, chemical or electrical burns, or any burn on a child or older adult.

## 6. Shock (pale, cold, clammy, fast weak pulse, confused)
- Lay them down, keep them warm, and stay with them. Nothing to eat or drink. Treat the cause (bleeding first). Get help.

## 7. Stroke: think FAST
**F**ace drooping · **A**rm weak or numb · **S**peech slurred · **T**ime to call 911. Note when it started. Don't give food, drink or medicine.

## 8. Severe allergic reaction (swelling, trouble breathing, hives, faintness)
- Use their **epinephrine auto-injector** (EpiPen) in the outer thigh, through clothing if needed. Call 911.
- If symptoms don't improve, a second dose can be given after 5–15 minutes if a second injector is available.

## 9. Broken bones and sprains
- Don't straighten it. **Splint it as found**, padding well, covering the joints above and below the injury.
- Check fingers or toes past the injury: still warm and pink? Loosen the splint if they go pale, blue, numb or cold.
- Suspected **neck or back** injury: don't move them unless they're in danger. Keep the head still.

## 10. Heat and cold
- **Heat stroke** (hot skin, confusion, fainting, may stop sweating): **emergency**. Cool them immediately, with cold water immersion if possible or water and fanning, and ice packs to neck, armpits and groin. Call 911.
- **Heat exhaustion** (heavy sweating, weak, dizzy, nausea): cool place, loosen clothes, sip water. Worse or vomiting → medical help.
- **Hypothermia** (shivering, slurred speech, clumsy, confused, sleepy): get them out of wind and wet, remove wet clothes, wrap in dry layers, warm the core (chest, neck, armpits). Warm sweet drinks only if fully awake. Handle gently.
- **Frostbite** (white or grayish, waxy, numb skin): don't rub it. Rewarm in warm (not hot) water, about 99–104°F / 37–40°C, **only if it won't freeze again**.

## 11. Head injuries: get help fast if…
Worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion or unusual sleepiness, one pupil bigger than the other, seizure, weakness on one side, or clear fluid from the nose or ears.

## 12. Poisoning, bites and stings
- **Poison Control (US): 1-800-222-1222.** Don't make them vomit unless told to.
- **Snakebite**: keep calm and still, keep the bitten limb at about heart level, remove rings and watches, get to a hospital. **No** tourniquet, cutting, sucking or ice.
- **Bee sting**: scrape the stinger out sideways. Watch for allergic reaction (see 8).

## 13. Wound care when help is far away
- Rinse with lots of clean (boiled or treated) water. Remove dirt. Cover with a clean dressing and change it daily.
- **Infection signs**: spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaks, fever. Red streaks or fever = needs antibiotics and medical care urgently.

Sources: American Heart Association CPR guidelines; American Red Cross First Aid; Stop the Bleed (American College of Surgeons); CDC heat, cold and frostbite guidance; US Army TC 4-02.1 First Aid. This card is a memory aid, not a substitute for training. Take a CPR and Stop the Bleed class.
