# Evacuation and Leaving the Country Card

## Leave early
The hardest part is deciding. Pick **triggers in advance** ("if the mandatory evacuation order comes," "if the bridge closes," "if fighting reaches X") and act on them. Leaving early beats traffic, empty gas stations, closed borders and panic.

## Go-bag (one per person, ready by the door)
- Water (at least 1 gallon / 4 L per person) and a filter or purification tablets
- 3 days of no-cook food
- Medications (2 weeks if possible, in original bottles), glasses, first-aid kit
- Phone, charger, battery pack, small battery or crank radio
- Flashlight or headlamp and spare batteries
- Change of clothes, rain jacket, warm layer, sturdy shoes, hat
- Cash in small bills, copies of documents (see below)
- Multi-tool, duct tape, lighter, whistle, N95 masks, garbage bags
- Paper map of your region with routes marked
- Kids: comfort item, snacks, a card with your name and phone number. Pets: leash, food, records.

## Documents (originals in a waterproof bag; copies on paper and an encrypted USB)
Passports and IDs · birth and marriage certificates · property deed or lease · insurance policies · bank and card contacts · medical records, prescriptions, vaccination records · pet records · a list of important phone numbers written on paper.

## Routes and meeting places
- Plan **three routes** out: primary, secondary, tertiary. Avoid choke points: bridges, tunnels, interstates.
- **Rally points**: (1) right outside the home, (2) somewhere in the neighborhood, (3) out of town.
- Pick an **out-of-area contact** everyone checks in with. **Texts often get through when calls don't.**
- Tell someone your route and expected arrival time.

## Leaving the country
- **Know your options before you need them**: citizenships, residency rights, and family abroad. A second passport is a powerful asset; keep it valid and in the go-bag.
- Many countries require your **passport to be valid 6 months beyond entry**. Check visas and entry authorizations for your destination.
- **US citizens must use a US passport to leave and enter the US.** Dual citizens generally enter their other country on that country's passport.
- Use **official border crossings and airports** only. Know the two or three nearest of each, and alternatives if the first closes.
- Commercial flights are the easiest way out. Book early; prices and availability get worse fast.
- Carry medications in original containers with copies of prescriptions. Check whether your medications are legal where you're going.
- **US citizens abroad**: enroll in the State Department's Smart Traveler Enrollment Program (STEP) so the embassy can reach you.
- Carry some cash in the destination's currency, and a card that works abroad.
- People fleeing danger have the right to seek asylum (UN Refugee Convention). The UNHCR and Red Cross help displaced families find each other.

## During unrest
- Avoid crowds, protests and gathering points. Travel in daylight, on routes with normal traffic.
- At official checkpoints: stay calm, keep hands visible, follow instructions, have documents ready.
- Don't photograph police, soldiers or checkpoints.
- Keep phone and battery packs charged. Download offline maps (or carry Beacon).
- Keep a low profile (see the Self-defense card).

Sources: FEMA / Ready.gov "Make a Plan"; US Department of State travel and STEP guidance; UNHCR; International Committee of the Red Cross.
