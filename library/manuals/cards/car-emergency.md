# Car Emergency Card

## Winter car kit (keep it in the trunk November–March)
Blanket or sleeping bag, hat and gloves, ice scraper and small shovel, sand or cat litter for traction, jumper cables, flashlight and spare batteries, phone charger, water and snacks, first-aid kit, bright cloth to tie on the antenna, and a candle in a metal can for a little heat and light (crack a window).

## Stuck in snow or stranded
1. **Stay with the car** unless help is visible and close. A car is easier to find than a person, and it's shelter.
2. **Clear the tailpipe of snow.** A blocked exhaust fills the car with carbon monoxide.
3. Run the engine about **10 minutes each hour** for heat, with a window cracked slightly.
4. Tie a bright cloth to the antenna or door. Turn the dome light on at night while the engine runs.
5. Keep moving your arms and legs to stay warm. Don't overexert yourself shoveling in deep cold.
6. Huddle together under blankets. Drink water; eat snacks.

## Jump-starting a dead battery
1. Both cars off. Red clamp on the **dead battery's +**, other red clamp on the **good battery's +**.
2. Black clamp on the **good battery's −**, last black clamp on **bare, unpainted metal on the dead car**, away from the battery.
3. Start the good car, wait a few minutes, then start the dead car.
4. Remove the clamps in reverse order. Drive at least 20–30 minutes to recharge.
- Manual transmission with no cables? A **push start** can work: ignition on, clutch in, 2nd gear, push to a jog, pop the clutch.

## Flat tire
1. Hazards on. Get fully off the road onto flat, firm ground. Parking brake on. Everyone out, away from traffic.
2. **Loosen the lug nuts a half-turn before jacking.**
3. Jack at the frame point shown in the owner's manual. Never put any part of your body under the car.
4. Swap the tire. Tighten the nuts in a **star pattern**. Recheck after a few miles.
- Small "donut" spares are usually limited to about 50 mph and 50–70 miles.

## Overheating
- Turn off the A/C and turn the **heater on full**: it pulls heat away from the engine. Pull over safely.
- **Never open a hot radiator cap**; it can spray scalding coolant. Wait at least 30 minutes.
- Top up coolant, or plain water in an emergency, only when the engine is cool.

## Water on the road
- **Never drive through flood water.** 12 inches of moving water can float most cars.
- **If your car goes into water: Seatbelts, Windows, Out, Children (SWOC).**
  1. Unbuckle. 2. Open or break a **side window** right away; the windshield won't break. 3. Get the children out first, oldest helping youngest. 4. Climb out and swim away. Don't wait for the car to fill to open a door.
  - Keep a window-breaker / seatbelt-cutter tool within reach of the driver.

## Brakes fail
Pump the brake pedal hard. Shift to a lower gear. Pull the parking brake **slowly and steadily**. Steer toward an uphill road, soft shoulder or open area. Use horn and hazards.

## Fuel and range
- Keep the tank **at least half full** in storm season or unrest. Gas stations need power to pump.
- Electric vehicles: charge above 80% ahead of storms. An EV can power phones and small devices for a long time; check what yours supports.
- Don't siphon fuel by mouth. Use a hand siphon pump. Gasoline vapors and liquid are poisonous.

## Breakdown checklist
Warning triangle or flares behind the car · stay inside, buckled, if on a busy highway shoulder · call for help · note the nearest mile marker or exit.

Sources: NWS / Ready.gov winter driving; NHTSA vehicle safety; Prof. Gordon Giesbrecht's vehicle-submersion research (SWOC); owner's manual procedures.
