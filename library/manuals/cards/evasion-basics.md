# Survive & Evade Card

For getting yourself and others to safety when moving openly isn't safe: unrest, conflict, or a hostile area. Adapted from the US military's public survival, evasion and recovery manuals.

## First: stay or go?
- **Shelter in place** if you have a safe, supplied hiding place and help is coming to you.
- **Move** when staying is more dangerous, when you know where safety is (a border, a friendly town, a rescue point) and you have a route, water and a plan.
- Decide your **trigger** for leaving in advance. Moving in a panic is when people get caught or hurt.

## Plan the route
- Avoid roads, towns, bridges, checkpoints and crowds when possible. Paths along the edges of woods, fields, ditches and streams are less watched.
- Don't walk along ridgelines or skylines, where your silhouette shows. Use dead ground, the low areas out of sight.
- Pick checkpoints along the way (a stream junction, a tower), rally points if the group splits, and a backup route.
- Travel when the area is quietest. Rest hidden during the most dangerous hours.

## Hide well: BLISS
Pick hiding places that are **B**lend in, **L**ow in silhouette, **I**rregular in shape, **S**mall and **S**ecluded. Avoid the obvious spot (the lone shed, the only thicket); searchers check those first.

## Don't be seen: the camouflage S's
**Shape** (break up your outline), **Shine** (cover anything reflective: watches, glasses, phone screens), **Shadow** (stay in it; mind your own), **Silhouette** (no skylines or open doorways), **Spacing** (spread out in irregular gaps, not neat lines), **Movement** (it's what eyes catch first: move slowly, freeze when unsure).

## Moving quietly
- Stop, look and listen every few minutes. Let your senses settle.
- Don't follow trails. Leave no trash, no fire, minimal tracks. Cross roads at a bend or a narrow spot, quickly, one at a time.
- **Light and noise discipline**: no phone screens, flashlights or smoking at night. Tape or tie down anything that rattles.
- Phones can reveal your location. If being tracked is a concern, keep the phone off and only turn it on briefly when you need it.

## Water, food and health on the move
- Water first: carry it, know where the next source is, treat it before drinking.
- Eat small amounts often. Look after your feet: dry socks, treat blisters early.
- Treat injuries as soon as it's safe; untreated wounds get infected fast.

## Signaling and recovery
- Signal only when you're sure it's friendly: rescuers, aid workers, a safe border post. See the Signals card.
- Approach checkpoints and borders openly, calmly, with hands visible and documents ready.

## Reaching safety across a border
- Use **official crossings** when you can. People fleeing danger have the right to ask for asylum.
- Carry documents (or copies), medication in original containers, and some local currency.
- **Never carry weapons across a border.**
- Registering with the UNHCR or Red Cross helps separated families find each other.

## Mindset
Patience keeps people alive. Make small plans you can complete, rest when you can, and remember who you're getting home to.

Sources: US Army FM 3-50.3 (Multi-Service Survival, Evasion and Recovery); Air Force AFH 10-644 SERE Operations; US Army ATP 3-50.21 Survival; UNHCR.
