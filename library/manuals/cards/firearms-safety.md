# Firearms & Archery Safety Card

## The four rules (always, every time)
1. **Treat every gun as if it's loaded.**
2. **Never point the muzzle at anything you aren't willing to destroy.**
3. **Keep your finger off the trigger** until your sights are on the target and you've decided to shoot.
4. **Be sure of your target and what's beyond it.** Bullets go through walls and travel a long way.

## Storage
- **Locked and out of reach of children and anyone in crisis**: a safe, lockbox, or cable or trigger lock. Children find hidden guns.
- Store ammunition separately when the gun isn't kept ready for defense. Use only the exact cartridge marked on the barrel.
- Every time you pick up a gun, **check that it's unloaded** (open the action, look and feel the chamber and magazine well), even if someone just handed it to you.

## Handling
- Eye and hearing protection when shooting. Know how your specific gun's safety, action and loading work (read its manual; many are free from the manufacturer).
- Clean and lightly oil after use, especially after rain or humidity.
- No alcohol, drugs or exhaustion around guns.

## The law
Ownership, transport, carry and self-defense laws vary by state and country, and they still apply in emergencies. Many countries ban or tightly restrict civilian guns. **Don't carry a firearm across a border** without permission from both countries.

## Archery
- Never "dry fire" a bow (releasing the string without an arrow); it can break the bow and injure you.
- Check arrows for cracks before each shot, especially carbon. Nock only when ready to shoot, pointed at the target.
- Know what's behind the target; arrows skip and glance.
- Hunting with a bow requires much closer range, about 20–40 yards for most hunters, and precise shot placement. Practice until you can group tightly.

## Train
A safety course (hunter education, NRA, or your local range) is the single best thing you can do. Hunter-ed manuals are free online in most states.

Sources: NSSF / NRA basic firearm safety rules; US Army TC 3-22.9 Rifle and Carbine; state hunter education programs.
