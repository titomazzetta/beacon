# Sanitation and Hygiene Card

After injuries, disease from dirty water and poor sanitation is what kills people in long disasters. Three habits prevent most of it: keep waste away from water, wash hands, keep flies off food.

## Wash hands
- Soap and water for 20 seconds: after the toilet, before food, after caring for someone sick, after trash or animals.
- No running water: a jug with a spout (or a can with a hole), soap, and a bucket underneath. Hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol works when hands aren't visibly dirty.

## Toilets without water
- If pipes may be damaged or the water is off, don't flush: sewage can back up into the house.
- **Bucket toilet**: line a bucket with a heavy trash bag. After each use, cover with sawdust, cat litter or dry dirt. Tie it off when half full, double-bag it, and keep it in a closed container until it can be collected or buried.
- Keeping urine in a separate bucket cuts the smell and the volume a lot.
- **Latrine for a group**: at least 200 feet (60 m) from any well, stream or water source and downhill from it, with a cover, and a handwashing station right next to it.

## Water and food
- Keep drinking water covered and separate. Pour it out or use a clean cup; never dip hands in.
- Cook food all the way through and eat it hot. Don't keep cooked leftovers without refrigeration.
- Wash dishes, then sanitize them: **1 tablespoon** of plain, unscented bleach per gallon of water, then air dry.

## Cleaning after floods and sickness
- Surfaces that touched floodwater (floors, sinks, tools): **1 cup of bleach per 5 gallons** of water, after cleaning. Mold on hard surfaces: **1 cup per gallon**.
- **Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner.** Open windows, wear gloves and eye protection.

## Trash, pests and flies
- Bag trash, keep it covered and away from where people eat and sleep. Burn only where it's safe and allowed, or bury it away from water.
- Empty anything that holds standing water: it breeds mosquitoes within days.

## Watch for disease
- Diarrhea: start the rehydration drink (**Tools > Rehydration drink**) early and keep sipping.
- Get help for blood in the stool, high fever, sunken eyes, very little urine, or large amounts of watery diarrhea that looks like rice water (a sign of cholera).
- Keep a sick person's dishes, towels and toilet separate if you can. Whoever cares for them washes hands every time.

## Body care
- Wash every day, even with just a damp cloth. Change into dry socks: wet feet for days cause trench foot.
- Keep wounds clean and covered. Brush teeth with safe water.
- Periods: keep extra pads, or a menstrual cup. Wash reusable cloths with soap and dry them in the sun.

Sources: CDC (handwashing; cleaning and sanitizing with bleach after an emergency; food and water safety); WHO; Sphere Handbook; US Army FM 4-25.12, Unit Field Sanitation Team.
