# Make Do Card: Using What's Around You

No kit? Look around first. Every home, car and campsite is full of things that solve problems. Work in this order: stay safe, stay warm or cool, get safe water, signal for help, then food.

## Water from almost nothing
- **Hidden water at home**: the water heater tank (turn off its power or gas first, then open the drain valve at the bottom), the toilet tank (not the bowl, and not if it has cleaning tablets), ice cubes, and the liquid in canned food. To drain the pipes, open the highest faucet in the house, then catch water from the lowest one.
- **Collect it**: rain from roofs, gutters, tarps or plastic sheeting into clean containers. Melt snow before drinking it; eating it chills you. Wipe dew off grass with a cloth and wring it out.
- **Clean it in two steps**. First make it clear: let it settle and pour off the top, then strain it through a clean cloth or coffee filter. A layered filter of cloth, sand and crushed charcoal makes water clearer, **not** safe. Then kill the germs:
  - **Boil** it for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 feet) in any metal pot or can.
  - **Bleach**: 8 drops of plain 6% bleach per gallon, wait 30 minutes (**Tools > Bleach for drinking water**).
  - **Sun (SODIS)**: fill clear plastic bottles, 2 liters or smaller, with clear water. Lay them in full sun for 6 hours, or 2 full days if it's cloudy.
- **Never drink** seawater, urine, water from a car radiator (antifreeze is poison), or pool and hot tub water.

## Warm
- Pick one small room with few windows. Close the doors, hang blankets over windows and doorways, and gather everyone, pets included.
- A table draped in blankets makes a tent inside the room that holds body heat.
- Wear loose layers, a hat and dry socks. Sleep off the floor on cardboard or cushions.
- Hot water in a bottle wrapped in a towel makes a safe bed warmer.
- **Never** use an oven, grill, camp stove or generator indoors to heat. Carbon monoxide kills quietly.

## Cool
- Shade, the lowest floor, and covered sunny windows. Wet cloths on the neck and wrists, and a fan blowing across a damp towel.
- Food: a cooler with ice; in cold weather, freeze jugs of water outside rather than burying food in snow. In dry heat, a clay pot inside a larger clay pot with wet sand between them (a zeer pot) keeps produce cool.

## Light and power
- Flashlights and headlamps first. Solar garden lights brought inside at night make free lamps. Candles only in sturdy holders, away from anything that burns, never left alone.
- Phones: low power mode and airplane mode, screen dim. In a group, keep one phone on at a time.
- Charge from the car (see the Satellite and car card), power banks, or a laptop's USB port.

## Cooking without power
- **Outdoors only**: a grill, camp stove or fire pit.
- A few bricks or cans can make a small "rocket stove" that cooks well on a handful of sticks.
- Save fuel: bring rice, grains or soaked beans to a boil, then wrap the closed pot in blankets or put it in a cooler to keep cooking. Bring it back to a boil before eating.
- Eat the fridge food first, then the freezer, then the pantry. Throw away swollen, leaking or badly dented cans.

## First aid with what's there
- **Bleeding**: press hard with a clean cloth, and pack deep wounds with cloth. An improvised tourniquet is a last resort if there's no real one: a band of cloth at least 2 inches (5 cm) wide, high on the limb, twisted tight with a stick until the bleeding stops. Write down the time. Never use wire or thin cord.
- **Splint**: rolled magazines, cardboard or sticks, padded with cloth and tied above and below the injury. A sling from a triangle of cloth, or a buttoned shirt.
- **Burns**: cool running water for at least 10 minutes, then cover loosely with clean plastic wrap or cloth.

## Signal
A whistle (three blasts), a mirror, CD or foil to flash sunlight, bright clothes or tarps laid out, big letters on the ground, three fires in a triangle. See the Signals card.

## Worth grabbing around the house
Trash bags (poncho, ground sheet, water catcher, toilet liner), duct tape, aluminum foil (reflects heat, cooks food, signals), plastic sheeting, plain bleach, buckets with lids, bandanas, string or cord, a sharp knife, a lighter or matches.

Sources: Ready.gov; CDC (emergency water, solar disinfection); WHO; USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service; American Red Cross; Stop the Bleed; US Army ATP 3-50.21 Survival.
