# Power Outage Card

## Carbon monoxide kills: read this first
- **Generators run outdoors only**, at least **20 feet** from doors, windows, and vents. Never in a garage, porch, or basement, even with the door open.
- **Never** heat the house with a gas oven, grill, charcoal, or camp stove.
- Keep **battery-powered CO alarms** on every floor. Headache, dizziness, nausea, or confusion in several people at once = get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911.

## Backup power
- **Never plug a generator into a wall outlet** ("backfeeding"). It can electrocute utility line workers and neighbors. Use heavy-duty extension cords to individual appliances, or a transfer switch installed by an electrician.
- **Inverters** don't burn fuel, so the inverter itself is safe indoors. If it runs off a **gas car**, the idling car must be outside in open air, never in an attached garage: its exhaust is carbon monoxide. An **EV** makes no exhaust.
- Prioritize: fridge (a few hours on, a few off is fine), phone charging, lights, medical devices, a furnace blower if it can be safely powered.

## Food safety
- Keep fridge and freezer **doors closed**. Fridge keeps food cold about **4 hours**. Full freezer about **48 hours**, half-full about **24 hours**.
- Throw out perishable food (meat, dairy, eggs, leftovers) that has been **above 40°F for 2 hours or more**. When in doubt, throw it out. Never taste-test.
- An appliance thermometer in the fridge and freezer tells you for sure.

## Staying warm (winter)
- Pick **one room**, ideally interior with few windows. Close doors and hang blankets over doorways and windows.
- Dress in **layers**, hat, dry socks. Sleep together under extra blankets. Eat and drink: your body needs fuel to make heat.
- **Hypothermia signs**: shivering, slurred speech, clumsiness, confusion, drowsiness. Get them dry and warm, warm sweet drinks if alert, and get medical help.

## Protect the house
- **Pipes**: let faucets on outside walls drip; open cabinet doors under sinks. If pipes freeze, never thaw with an open flame.
- Unplug electronics to protect from surges when power returns. Leave one lamp on so you know when it's back.

Sources: CDC carbon monoxide guidance; USDA FSIS "Keeping Food Safe During an Emergency"; Ready.gov Power Outages and Winter Weather; OSHA portable generator safety.
