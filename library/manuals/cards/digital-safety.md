# Digital Safety & OPSEC Card

Phones keep people alive in a crisis. They can also give away where you are, who you know and what you have. Set this up before you need it.

## Lock the phone down
- Use a long passcode (six or more characters, not 123456). When you might be stopped, switch off Face ID or fingerprint: in many places people can be forced to unlock with a face or finger more easily than with a passcode.
  - iPhone: hold the side button and a volume button for two seconds; Face ID stays off until the passcode is entered.
  - Android: turn on "Show lockdown option" in lock screen settings, then choose Lockdown from the power menu.
- Phones are encrypted when a passcode is set. On a Mac, turn on FileVault.
- For high-risk situations, iPhone has Lockdown Mode (Settings → Privacy & Security).
- Hide message previews on the lock screen. Do keep a medical ID and emergency contacts there.

## Location and tracking
- A phone that's on talks to cell towers, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth even with location services off. Airplane mode cuts most of that; powered off, or in a signal-blocking (Faraday) bag, cuts all of it.
- Don't post where you are or where you're going in real time. Photos can carry their GPS location: share screenshots instead, or turn off location for the camera.
- Share live location only with people who need it, and turn it off if a phone might be taken.

## Messages
- Use Signal for calls and texts (end-to-end encrypted). Turn on disappearing messages for sensitive chats and set a registration lock PIN.
- Agree on simple code words in advance: "we're leaving," "don't come," "I'm not free to talk."
- Radios and mesh radios use public airwaves: anyone nearby can listen. Use private channels, and never say addresses, supplies, or when a house will be empty.

## Checkpoints and borders
- Carry as little data as you can. A separate travel phone with only what you need is safest. Back up first, and keep the backup somewhere else.
- Turn devices fully off before a checkpoint or border. Log out of sensitive apps, or remove them; just deleting files may not erase them.
- Know the local rules: in some places refusing to unlock has serious consequences. Your safety comes first.

## Scams after disasters
- Scammers pose as aid groups, insurers, contractors and officials. Check them through official websites or numbers you already have. Never pay with gift cards, wire transfers or crypto.

## Accounts and documents
- Use a password manager, and two-step sign-in with an authenticator app or a security key rather than text messages.
- Keep copies of key documents (IDs, insurance, prescriptions) on this drive, encrypted, and with someone you trust far away.
