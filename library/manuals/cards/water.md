# Safe Drinking Water Quick Card

## How much
- Store **1 gallon per person per day** (drinking plus basic hygiene). Keep at least 3 days; 2 weeks is better. Add extra for pets and hot weather.

## Hidden water already in your house
- **Water heater tank** (turn off power or gas first, let it cool, drain from the valve at the bottom).
- **Ice cubes**, canned fruit and vegetable liquid.
- **Toilet tank** (the back tank, NOT the bowl), only if no chemical cleaner tablets are in it. Treat before drinking.
- Pipes: open the highest faucet in the house, then drain from the lowest one.

## Method 1: Boil (best)
1. If water is cloudy, let it settle and pour off the clear water, or filter through a clean cloth or coffee filter.
2. Bring to a **rolling boil for 1 minute**. Above 6,500 feet elevation, boil for **3 minutes**.
3. Let it cool. Store in clean, covered containers.

## Method 2: Household bleach (when you can't boil)
Use **plain, unscented** bleach that's **less than a year old** (it weakens over time). No splash-less, scented, or color-safe types. Check the label for the strength:

| Bleach strength | Per 1 gallon of clear water |
|---|---|
| 6% sodium hypochlorite | **8 drops** (about 1/8 teaspoon) |
| 8.25% sodium hypochlorite | **6 drops** |

- **Cloudy or very cold water**: filter cloudy water first if you can, and double the amount.
- Stir, then **wait 30 minutes**. It should smell slightly of chlorine. If not, repeat the dose and wait 15 more minutes.

## What treatment does NOT fix
- Boiling and bleach kill germs. They do **not** remove chemicals, fuel, or heavy metals. If water may be contaminated by flood water mixed with fuel or chemicals, find another source.

## Signs of trouble after drinking bad water
- Diarrhea, vomiting, cramps, fever. The main danger is **dehydration**. Oral rehydration: 1 liter of safe water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + 1/2 level teaspoon salt (WHO/UNICEF home recipe). Get medical help for blood in stool, high fever, confusion, or a child or elderly person who can't keep fluids down.

Sources: EPA "Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water"; CDC "Making Water Safe in an Emergency"; Ready.gov Water; WHO/UNICEF oral rehydration guidance.
